# Wingman Competitive Landscape — v0.2.x Reality Check

**Date:** 2026-07-18. **Method:** vendor docs, pricing pages, changelogs, GitHub repos, MCP registries, and one primary-source LinkedIn ToS check, cross-referenced against third-party reviews only where primary sources were unavailable (flagged inline). Full per-tool backing evidence, with **[fact]**/**[inference]** labels and access dates, lives in the companion file `comparator-rubric-2026-07-18.md`. This memo answers the briefing's three questions — where Wingman is immature, where it duplicates a mature market, and where the next versions should go — against the manifesto invariants only, per the user's 2026-07-18 scoping direction (data residency and fetch mechanics are explicitly out of scope for scoring).

## 1. The invariants, restated as scoring criteria

Every comparator below is scored against these five bullets, and only these (per Wingman's own research briefing and VISION.md, as directed by the user on 2026-07-18):

1. Optimizes opportunity **quality**, not application **volume**.
2. **Evidence before assertion** — every model claim needs a verbatim quote from a stored document; zero survivors, nothing stored.
3. **Human approval before external action** — Wingman never sends (RFC-006).
4. **Deterministic composition** over model claims where possible (dossiers, similarity, diffs, warmth are arithmetic, not generation).
5. **No invented familiarity**; partial truth beats polished fiction.

Two market facts make these criteria unusually load-bearing right now, not just aspirational branding. First, the dominant job-search product motion in 2026 is volume, not quality: Simplify alone reports "200,000,000+ applications submitted" through its autofill tool ([Simplify.jobs](https://simplify.jobs/)), and a parallel "auto-apply" tier (FastApply, Resumly, JobGPT) exists specifically to remove the human click from that loop entirely ([FastApply vs Teal, blog.fastapply.co, 2026](https://blog.fastapply.co/fastapply-vs-teal-review-and-comparison-2026); [Resumly vs Simplify, resumly.ai, 2026](https://www.resumly.ai/compare/resumly-vs-simplify)). Second, evidence discipline is not a solved problem elsewhere in the adjacent AI-productivity market: a widely circulated user report documents Notion AI fabricating source links and misattributing a movement's founder, with "less than 50% success rate" on cited links resolving to anything real ([Notion AI hallucination reports, Reddit, 2026 access](https://www.reddit.com/r/Notion/comments/10wtbeu/anyone_else_have_notion_ai_just_make_things_up/)), and Humantic AI's own Terms of Service explicitly disclaims any obligation to keep its personality-inference data current or accurate, calling its own insights "only suggestive in nature" ([Humantic AI Terms of Service](https://humantic.ai/tos)). Wingman's invariants are not table stakes the market already provides quietly — they are a real, currently-undefended position.

## 3. Where the manifesto is currently undefended vs. defended

Cross-referencing the matrix against the invariants surfaces a clean three-way split, which is the real input to the verdict buckets in Section 4.

**Autonomy posture is where the market is most polarized, and Wingman sits at the disciplined pole.** One cluster — Waalaxy, Dripify, JobGPT's auto-apply tools, community LinkedIn-scraping MCP servers — is explicitly built to act without a human click, up to and including outreach send. Dripify's own marketing describes "human behavior simulation" and rotating local IPs specifically to evade LinkedIn's abuse detection ([Dripify pricing](https://dripify.com/pricing/)), which is a vendor admitting, in its own sales copy, that its core value proposition requires evading the platform it operates on. A second cluster — Teal, Simplify, Rezi (including its MCP server), Four-Leaf's deep-link pattern — has converged independently on "assist, never auto-execute," which validates Wingman's RFC-006 as a defensible, non-eccentric position rather than a growth-limiting one; multiple unrelated vendors have landed on the same human-in-the-loop line without any coordination, suggesting it is closer to a durable market equilibrium than a Wingman-only constraint.

**Evidence discipline is almost nowhere formalized as a product guarantee**, and where it is discussed at all, the market's honest position is a disclaimer, not a solution. Humantic AI's Terms of Service is the sharpest data point: it explicitly states no obligation to validate or refresh its data and calls its own personality insights "only suggestive in nature," with all usage risk assigned to the customer ([Humantic AI ToS](https://humantic.ai/tos)). Notion AI's user-reported link fabrication shows what happens when a mainstream, well-funded product ships a generative claims layer without a verbatim-quote gate ([Notion AI hallucination reports](https://www.reddit.com/r/Notion/comments/10wtbeu/anyone_else_have_notion_ai_just_make_things_up/)). No comparator surveyed enforces "zero verbatim-quote survivors → nothing stored" as a hard product rule the way Wingman's briefing describes it. This is Wingman's most defensible and least contested differentiator.

**Deterministic composition is a spectrum, and Wingman is not alone on the deterministic end, but it is alone in applying that discipline to career/company intelligence specifically.** changedetection.io's default hash-and-diff engine is fully deterministic, with an LLM summarization layer offered as an *optional* add-on, not the default ([changedetection.io](https://github.com/dgtlmoon/changedetection.io)) — this is philosophically the closest analog to Wingman's own "arithmetic first, generation only where unavoidable" stance, but changedetection.io applies it to generic web monitoring, not to company dossiers or person POV cards. Feedly's AI Feeds and Inoreader's Intelligence layer are explicitly model-based for relevance filtering ([Feedly AI Feed docs](https://docs.feedly.com/article/764-what-is-an-ai-feed-feedly)), the opposite choice from what a strict reading of Wingman's invariant would make for its own news-relevance problem — worth noting as external validation that model-based relevance filtering is an accepted, common design choice industry-wide, which somewhat softens the case that Wingman's heuristic relevance filter is a competitive liability rather than an invariant-preserving tradeoff.

## 4. Verdict buckets — feature areas A–F

### A. Profile & corpus — **KEEP THIN**

Resume ingestion across MD/text/PDF/DOCX/LaTeX/Google Docs plus a personal-corpus FTS5 index is real, necessary infrastructure, but it is not where Wingman's invariants create categorical distance from the market. Rezi, Teal, and Huntr all have mature resume-import and multi-version resume pipelines that are objectively more polished as *resume products* — Rezi's "62% of users land an interview" positioning and unlimited AI resume generation ([Rezi pricing](https://www.rezi.ai/pricing)) reflect years of UX investment Wingman shouldn't try to match feature-for-feature. What none of them do is treat the resume as a *cited evidentiary corpus* feeding downstream claims (opportunity verdicts, POV-card generation, outreach briefs) rather than as a document to optimize and export. That downstream use is the actual differentiator, and it depends on A existing, cheaply and reliably, as plumbing — not on A being best-in-class as a standalone resume builder. Obsidian's plugin API and local-Markdown model are the closest philosophical fit for how Wingman should think about corpus storage if it ever needs to borrow ideas, but nothing here is worth a rebuild-vs-adopt debate; A stays a minimal internal component in service of B–E.

### B. Opportunity assessment — **BUILD (versus)**

This is the area furthest from any comparator surveyed, and the invariant that makes it different is specific: JD-to-requirement extraction with a verbatim quote requirement, producing met/partial/gap/unknown verdicts citing only real profile items. Every comparator in the space does something adjacent but categorically weaker. Jobscan and Huntr report percentage match scores and keyword gaps — useful, but a match-rate number is not a per-requirement, quote-cited verdict, and neither product claims otherwise ([Jobscan pricing teardown, atsresumeai.com](https://www.atsresumeai.com/compare/is-jobscan-worth-it); [Huntr pricing](https://huntr.co/pricing)). Four-Leaf's `match_score` tool is the most sophisticated comparator found — "a real scoring algorithm... returning a 0-100 fit number plus skills, experience, and role-alignment breakdowns" ([Four-Leaf MCP writeup](https://dev.to/fourleaf/how-we-built-a-job-search-assistant-mcp-for-claude-cursor-and-chatgpt-13d2)) — but it is still a score, not a quote-anchored ledger a user can audit line by line. No incumbent surveyed can "follow" into Wingman's version without adopting the same all-or-nothing evidentiary discipline, which would mean abandoning the score-based UX their whole product is built around. This is exactly the kind of feature the briefing anticipated verifying, and it verifies clean: keep building it, and treat the verdict format itself (not just the underlying matching) as the moat.

### C. People intelligence — **BUILD (versus), with one KEEP-THIN carve-out for feed mechanics**

The core of C — POV cards with verbatim quotes, a deterministic warmth score, recommendation-graph discovery — has no real comparator. The sales-intelligence category (Humantic, Crystal) does the closest adjacent thing, personality inference from public data, and it is the single clearest negative case study in this whole research pass: Humantic's ToS explicitly disclaims accuracy and currency guarantees on the exact kind of person-level insight Wingman's POV cards are designed to make trustworthy by construction ([Humantic ToS](https://humantic.ai/tos)). Personal-CRM tools (Clay.earth/Mesh, Dex, folk) solve the *watchlist and reminders* half of C well, and Mesh in particular already auto-aggregates job-change and news signals in a way that overlaps Wingman's "news snapshots per person" ambition ([clay.earth](https://clay.earth/)) — but none of them compute a disclosed, deterministic warmth formula or extract quote-cited stances. The one place to be honest about market maturity: feed *discovery and reading mechanics* (RSS/Atom parsing, JS-only-site handling, relevance tagging) is a solved, commoditized problem — Inoreader explicitly handles "Web feeds & Track changes" for sites without RSS, the exact known-immature gap Wingman's own briefing flags ([Inoreader pricing](https://www.inoreader.com/pricing)) — so the mechanical feed-ingestion layer under C is a candidate for INTEGRATE-OR-DROP treatment (see Section 5), even while the POV/warmth/similarity logic built on top of those feeds stays BUILD.

### D. Company intelligence — **BUILD (versus) for dossiers/themes; INTEGRATE for the page-diff mechanics; INTEGRATE for raw company facts**

This area splits three ways and the briefing's own hypothesis (test changedetection.io/Visualping against RFC-015) turns out to be correct but incomplete — it's not just RFC-015's mechanics that have a mature substitute, so does some of D's raw-data layer. The [fact]/[inference]-labeled dossier and quote-validated company-theme synthesis (RFC-016) remain BUILD: Crunchbase and Harmonic supply structured facts and PitchBook supplies deeply curated private-market data, but none of them produce an author-attributed, quote-validated synthesis over a company's own people's writing the way Wingman's themes engine does — that synthesis layer is genuinely novel and depends on the evidence invariant to be trustworthy. But the underlying *page-monitoring mechanism* in RFC-015 ("user-named pages, one GET each, snapshot = text hash + link set, findings = N new links since date") is, feature-for-feature, already built better and for free by changedetection.io: deterministic hash/diff, full version history, RSS reader mode, trigger/extract-text filters, webhook output, all self-hostable at zero cost ([changedetection.io](https://github.com/dgtlmoon/changedetection.io)). And for raw company facts (funding, headcount, leadership), Crunchbase's Basic/Pro API is cheap, well-documented (token auth, 200 calls/minute, JSON entities) and already fresher/broader than anything Wingman could maintain in-house at low cost ([Crunchbase API docs](https://data.crunchbase.com/docs/using-the-api)). The efficient design is: consume changedetection.io (or its API) for the diffing substrate, optionally pull Crunchbase facts as dossier inputs, and reserve Wingman's own engineering effort for the [fact]/[inference] labeling and quote-validated theme synthesis that sits on top of both.

### E. Outreach support — **BUILD (versus)**

E is the area where the market's dominant pattern is the precise opposite of Wingman's invariant, which makes the BUILD case almost definitional rather than comparative. Apollo, Waalaxy, and Dripify are all outreach-automation products whose core value is reducing human involvement in sending — Dripify's own marketing describes anti-detection features designed to make automated LinkedIn activity indistinguishable from a human's ([Dripify pricing](https://dripify.com/pricing/)), which is close to the opposite of "drafting support ends at material the user composes in their own voice." Careerflow's and Simplify's AI email writers are closer in spirit (generate a draft, human sends) but neither cites a POV stance or a corpus quote as a hard requirement for what the draft can claim — they are generic personalization, not evidence-gated personalization. Wingman's purpose-typed briefs (introduction/reconnection/job/advice) that must cite a POV stance and quote the user's own corpus verbatim are not available anywhere in the comparator set, and — as with B — no incumbent can adopt this without rearchitecting around a stricter evidentiary gate than their current generative-drafting products use.

### F. Delivery & orchestration — **BUILD for the MCP/CLI architecture; KEEP THIN for PDF rendering and backup/restore**

The MCP career-tool ecosystem is real but small (an estimated fewer than 10 dedicated servers among 3,000+ MCP servers registry-wide as of an April 2026 count, per one vendor's own comparison — [Workopia MCP comparison](https://workopia.io/blog/best-free-mcp-servers-job-search-2026)) and skews toward exactly the autonomy posture Wingman rejects: JobGPT's MCP server explicitly ships auto-apply and auto-send-outreach as named tool categories ([JobGPT MCP setup guide](https://6figr.com/blog/jobgpt-mcp-claude-code-646)), and community LinkedIn-scraping MCP servers operate via unofficial API access that LinkedIn's own User Agreement §8.2 prohibits ([LinkedIn Prohibited Software policy](https://www.linkedin.com/help/linkedin/answer/a1341387)). The two servers closest to Wingman's own posture — Rezi's (session-scoped OAuth, five tools, zero auto-apply/auto-send tools) and Four-Leaf's (deep-link-back-to-app instead of server-side execution for paid actions) — validate the *direction* (scoped auth, no autonomous-action tools) without approaching Wingman's breadth (33 tools, CLI parity, stdio + loopback HTTP + capability token). No comparator combines Wingman's specific F-area architecture; this stays BUILD. Inside F, though, two sub-components are generic infrastructure with no invariant-driven reason to build in-house: Letter PDF rendering (a templating/rendering problem any of a dozen document-generation libraries or services solve) and tarball backup/restore (a solved problem in any backup tool). These should stay thin, deliberately unglamorous, internal utilities — exactly the KEEP-THIN pattern the briefing anticipated for "PDF rendering."

## 5. INTEGRATE-OR-DROP shortlist, with the RFC-018-relevant integration detail

Per the briefing's request for enough interface detail to draft an RFC-018-style decision, three integrations stand out as ready to specify now:

**changedetection.io for RFC-015's mechanical substrate.** Self-hosted (Docker, pip, or an $8.99/month hosted option with proxies), no API key required for self-hosted use, output via RSS reader mode or webhook (Discord/Email/Slack/Telegram/webhook), full version-history diff archive already built ([changedetection.io](https://github.com/dgtlmoon/changedetection.io)). Data shape: per-monitor text hash + diff view + optional extracted-text filters. Recommended integration path: run it as a sidecar process Wingman's `make-it-so` pipeline calls into (webhook receiver or scheduled poll of its own API/DB), rather than reimplementing hash-and-diff logic; keep RFC-015's "N new links since date" *framing* as a thin Wingman-side interpretation layer over changedetection.io's raw diff output. This preserves the dossier-synthesis value (BUILD) while dropping the diffing-engine maintenance burden (INTEGRATE).

**Crunchbase Basic/Pro API for company-fact grounding.** Auth via `X-cb-user-key` header or `user_key` URL param; rate limit 200 calls/minute (25/minute on Basic-tier ODM plans); JSON entity responses for organizations, people, funding rounds ([Crunchbase API docs](https://data.crunchbase.com/docs/using-the-api); [Crunchbase Basic API FAQ](https://support.crunchbase.com/hc/en-us/articles/8327794377235-Crunchbase-Basic-API-FAQ)). Pro is $49/month billed annually with a 7-day trial ([Crunchbase pricing](https://support.crunchbase.com/hc/en-us/articles/360019482733-How-much-does-Crunchbase-cost)) — cheap enough to justify as a dossier input source cited as [fact] with a Crunchbase URL, rather than Wingman re-deriving funding/headcount facts from its own page-diff research alone.

**Application-status tracking: confirmed DROP, not just hypothesized.** The briefing's own hypothesis — that users pair Wingman with an external tracker rather than wanting one built in — holds up against the evidence. Every job-CRM comparator surveyed (Teal, Huntr, Simplify, Careerflow) treats the Kanban-style application tracker as a mature, commodity, free-tier-included feature; Huntr's free tier alone tracks 100 jobs, and Teal's tracker is unlimited even for free users ([Huntr pricing](https://huntr.co/pricing); [Teal pricing](https://www.tealhq.com/pricing)). Building a competing tracker would be pure feature-parity work in a category where free incumbents already win on polish, and it is explicitly outside Wingman's "find fewer, better opportunities" north star, which has nothing to say about post-application status bookkeeping. Recommendation: document a one-way export (e.g., a CSV/Notion-compatible export of the opportunities Wingman has assessed as worth pursuing) so users can hand off into whichever tracker they already use, and stop there.

**Resume keyword/ATS scoring: DROP the standalone product, keep the underlying JD-parsing.** Jobscan's whole business is the resume-to-JD match-rate score ([Jobscan pricing teardown](https://www.atsresumeai.com/compare/is-jobscan-worth-it)), and Wingman's opportunity-assessment engine already has to parse the JD for its own verdict pipeline — so there's no case for building a *separate* percentage-match feature to compete with Jobscan head-on; if a numeric score is ever wanted, derive it as a byproduct of the existing verdict data rather than a new capability.

## 6. Version-shaping memo

Search improvement is already queued first for v0.3, per the backlog, and this research sharpens rather than changes that sequencing: the market evidence shows unified search across corpus + people + companies + news + research has no direct analog to borrow — Feedly and Inoreader solve feed-level relevance well but neither indexes across a career-intelligence entity graph the way Wingman needs to, so this remains bespoke work, appropriately prioritized first.

**v0.3 — three bets, given search is already committed:**
1. *Ship the changedetection.io integration for RFC-015's diffing substrate*, freeing engineering time from maintaining a bespoke hash/diff engine and letting v0.3 spend that time on the B-area verdict UX instead, since B is confirmed as the sharpest BUILD-versus differentiator in this research. **Not being built:** a competing general-purpose page-monitoring engine — changedetection.io's free, self-hosted, actively maintained project already exceeds RFC-015's stated scope.
2. *Add a Crunchbase-sourced fact layer to company dossiers*, cited as [fact] alongside Wingman's own [inference] labels, closing a real gap (dossiers currently rely entirely on approved-source page-diffs and the company's own people's writing, with no independent funding/headcount ground truth). **Not being built:** an in-house company-facts crawler — Crunchbase's $49/month Pro API is cheap enough that building a competing facts database would be pure waste.
3. *Ship a one-way opportunity export* (CSV or similar) so Wingman's "small number of exceptional opportunities" can flow into whatever external tracker (Teal, Huntr, a spreadsheet) the user already has. **Not being built:** an in-app status-tracking Kanban board — every job-CRM comparator surveyed already gives this away free, and it has no connection to the opportunity-quality north star.

**v0.4 — three bets:**
1. *Interview preparation*, closing the briefing's own known-immature gap. This research found no comparator that does interview prep with Wingman's evidence discipline — Careerflow and Rezi both ship generic AI mock-interview features ([Careerflow: How It Works](https://www.careerflow.ai/how-it-works); [Rezi Chrome Extension docs](https://www.rezi.ai/rezi-docs/rezi-chrome-extension)) with no quote-cited grounding in the company's actual themes/dossier or the user's own POV-matched talking points — so this is a genuine BUILD opportunity that reuses B, D, and E's existing evidence infrastructure rather than starting fresh. **Not being built:** a generic mock-interview Q&A bank — Rezi and Careerflow already do that adequately for the commodity case.
2. *Warmth-score enrichment via a bounded, user-approved interaction-history import* (e.g., a one-time calendar or email metadata import, read-only, no live sync), addressing the briefing's flagged "3-signal, no interaction history" gap without violating the never-sends/human-approval invariant, since import is a one-time user-initiated action, not an ongoing autonomous fetch. **Not being built:** live email/calendar monitoring or a general personal-CRM replacement — Clay.earth/Mesh and Dex already do continuous relationship-signal aggregation well, and matching that continuously-synced breadth would mean competing with venture-funded personal-CRM products on their own turf rather than improving Wingman's specific warmth formula.
3. *Feed-discovery hardening for JS-only sites*, using headless-rendering techniques already proven by the mature feed-reading incumbents rather than inventing a new approach, since Inoreader's "Web feeds & Track changes" for non-RSS sites confirms this is a solved, not research-grade, problem ([Inoreader pricing](https://www.inoreader.com/pricing)). **Not being built:** a general-purpose website-to-RSS conversion product — that's Inoreader's RSS Builder and changedetection.io's RSS Reader Mode already, and either could plausibly be integrated instead of rebuilt if v0.4 capacity is tight (a candidate for re-evaluation at v0.5).

**v0.5 — three bets:**
1. *Action prioritization*, the second unbuilt vision item flagged in the briefing, now buildable on top of v0.3's opportunity export and v0.4's interview prep — i.e., a deterministic ranking/surfacing layer over the evidence Wingman has already assembled (verdicts, warmth, dossier recency), not a new generative feature. **Not being built:** a general task-manager or CRM-style action queue — that would drift toward the "status-tracking CRM" Wingman's manifesto explicitly rejects.
2. *A documented RFC-018 integration surface for Crunchbase and changedetection.io as first-class, user-configurable data sources* (not just internal plumbing), so power users can point Wingman at their own Visualping/changedetection.io instance or bring their own Crunchbase key — turning v0.3's internal integrations into an extensibility story consistent with F's MCP-first architecture. **Not being built:** first-party connectors to sales-intelligence platforms (Apollo, Clay.com, Harmonic) — their autonomy-forward, send-capable design and enterprise pricing make them poor fits for an individual-user, never-sends tool, and integrating them would import exactly the send-capable surface area Wingman's invariants exist to avoid.
3. *A minimal read-only web UI for the two or three highest-value views (opportunity verdicts, company dossiers)*, addressing the "no UI beyond CLI/MCP/PDF" gap for the subset of users for whom that is the actual adoption blocker, while deliberately not building a general application shell. **Not being built:** a full web application with in-app editing, notifications, or scheduling — that would reopen the "no UI by design" tradeoff the briefing flags as worth pressure-testing, not abandoning outright; v0.5 should test the read-only case before committing further.

Across all three versions, the throughline the evidence supports is: spend v0.3–v0.5 engineering effort on the handful of areas (B, E, interview prep, action prioritization, warmth) where the manifesto invariants create a moat no comparator can cross without abandoning their own business model, and spend as little as possible on the areas (page-diff mechanics, company facts, application tracking, resume keyword scoring, feed parsing) where mature, cheap, or free incumbents already do the job and Wingman's invariants have nothing distinctive to add.
