# Career ladder rubrics

Research commissioned to resolve **Q1** of
[`../../QUESTION-BLOCKS-DESIGN.md`](../../QUESTION-BLOCKS-DESIGN.md): where a
rubric's content comes from, and how its provenance stays honest.

- [`findings-perplexity-2026-08-22.md`](findings-perplexity-2026-08-22.md) —
  what is actually published by whom, under what terms, and how far the
  dimensions converge.

## What it settled

1. **No first-party public engineering ladder exists** for Google, Meta,
   Amazon, Apple, Netflix or Microsoft. Job qualifications and culture posts
   are not frameworks. A rubric branded with one of those names can never be
   better than a reconstruction.
2. **It does not need to be.** Dropbox publishes its complete framework under
   **Apache-2.0** from a Dropbox-owned repository (verified at source). That
   makes a genuinely `first_party`, redistributable rubric possible, which is
   strictly better than any reconstruction.
3. **Licence is a required field.** "Publicly readable" is not "ours to
   redistribute", and catalogue metadata proved unreliable — one catalogue
   listed Dropbox as unlicensed. Verify at the primary source.
4. **The five proposed dimensions are real**, with one correction: scope and
   business impact are separate axes in both Dropbox and GitLab, and merging
   them is the conflation that over-positions strong individual contributors.
   The shipped rubric now splits `reach` from `impact`.

Q1's remaining thread — job postings' own levelling language as a
per-employer first-party source — is open but not blocking; postings state
years-of-experience far more often than they describe scope.
